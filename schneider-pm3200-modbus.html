Schneider PM3250 / PM3255 Modbus Register Map (PM3200 Series)

How to read a Schneider Electric PM3200-series DIN-rail power meter over Modbus RTU. Only two of the four models have Modbus at all, the documented register is one more than the address on the wire, the meter knows FC03 and FC16 only, and energy comes twice — as an exact 64-bit integer and as a 32-bit float that quietly loses precision on a big feeder. This page covers all of it, from Schneider's current user manual.

Which PM3200 Models Have Modbus

All four measure through CTs (1 A or 5 A secondaries) and optionally VTs. If the front says PM3200 or PM3210, no Modbus setting will make it answer — it has no port.

No FC04, no FC06. Everything is a holding register read with FC03; an input-register read (FC04) is refused. A write of even one register must be FC16 with a quantity of 1 — masters that default to FC06 for single writes get an error. The yellow LED flashes while the meter communicates (also on a line error) and is off when nothing talks to it.

Register = Address + 1

Schneider's register tables give both numbers, and the manual states the rule outright: the address (hex) is what goes in the Modbus frame, and register = address + 1. Phase-1 current is register 3000 at address 0x0BB7 = 2999. A tool that takes 0-based addresses wants 2999; one with 1-based addressing wants 3000. Ask a 0-based tool for 3000 and every Float32 comes back as half of one value and half of the next.

The values are IEEE-754 Float32 in two registers. The manual does not spell out the word order; on this meter, as on other Schneider meters, the high word comes first — a phase voltage reading near 230 confirms it, a value like 1.2e-38 means the words are swapped.

Power Factor: the −2 to +2 Register

Registers 3078–3084 do not hold a plain power factor. Schneider encodes the quadrant into the number: between 0 and +1 is Q1 (active power positive, inductive), between +1 and +2 is Q4 (positive, capacitive), between −1 and 0 is Q3 and between −2 and −1 is Q2 (active power negative). A reading of 1.08 is a 0.92 capacitive power factor: PF = 2 − 1.08. Below −1 it is −2 − x. Show the raw register on an HMI and a perfectly good 0.92 leading load looks like an impossible 1.08.

Energy: Int64 or Float32

The PM3200 gives every energy counter twice, both in Wh (not kWh):

The Int64 value is exact. The Float32 has a 24-bit mantissa, so above 224 Wh — 16.8 MWh, a few months on a busy feeder — it can no longer count single watt-hours, and the step grows as the counter does: 2 Wh steps from 16.8 MWh, 4 Wh from 33.6 MWh, 64 Wh around a gigawatt-hour. For billing or sub-metering read the Int64. ModbusManager has no 64-bit data type, so the workspace reads the four words as UInt16 and combines them in its script: ((w0 × 65536 + w1) × 65536 + w2) × 65536 + w3.

Writing: the Command Interface

Settings are not written into their own registers. The meter has a command interface: write the command number to 5250 and its parameters from 5252 with one FC16, then read the result at 5376 (0 = valid; 3000 invalid command, 3001 invalid parameter, 3002 wrong number of parameters, 3007 not performed). Examples from the manual: 1003 sets date and time, 2060 selects the multi-tariff mode (1 = controlled over communication), 2008 sets the active tariff 1–4 — only when the tariff is under communication control. The active tariff can be read at 4191. Wiring, CT and VT ratios are set on the display; they are not in the Modbus list.

PM3255 Extras

  • Digital input status at 8905 (bitmap, 2 registers): DI1 and DI2, open / closed. Digital output status at 9667.
  • Logs (power demand, energy per day / week / month) through function 20 (0x14) Read File Record, file number 1.
  • Input metering: pulse counters on DI1 / DI2 at 3558 / 3562 (Int64) or 45202 / 45204 (Float32), e.g. a water or gas meter.

Common Mistakes

  • No answer at all: it is a PM3200 or PM3210 — no Modbus port. Or 8N2 on the master: the meter always uses one stop bit.
  • Values look like 1e-38 or 4e+9: the read started on the wrong register (3000 instead of 2999 on a 0-based tool), or the words are swapped.
  • "Illegal function": FC04 or FC06. Use FC03 and FC16.
  • Power factor above 1: the four-quadrant format; 2 − x.
  • Energy a thousand times too big: it is Wh, not kWh.
  • Energy that moves in steps of 2 or 4: Float32 above 16.8 MWh; read the Int64 at 3204.
  • A tariff write is ignored: the multi-tariff mode is not set to communication control (command 2060).

Step-by-Step: Reading a PM3250 with ModbusManager

  1. On the meter's display set the address, baud rate and parity (one stop bit is fixed).
  2. Wire a USB–RS-485 adapter to D0, D1 and 0 V; terminate the line at both ends.
  3. In ModbusManager choose RTU, the COM port and the same baud rate and parity.
  4. Poll FC03 from address 2999, quantity 8, as Float32: I1, I2, I3 and In in amps.
  5. Open the workspace below to get all values named and scaled, the power factor decoded and the exact energy counter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Schneider PM3200 have Modbus?

Only the PM3250 and PM3255 do, on RS-485 (Modbus RTU). The PM3200 and PM3210 have no communication port; the PM3210 has a pulse output.

What is the Modbus address of the PM3250 total active power?

Register 3060, which is address 3059 on the wire (FC03, Float32, kW). Schneider's rule is register = address + 1, so phase-1 current, register 3000, is address 2999.

Why does the PM3250 power factor read more than 1?

Registers 3078–3084 use a four-quadrant format from −2 to +2. A value between 1 and 2 is a capacitive power factor of 2 minus the value: 1.08 means 0.92 leading.

Should I read PM3200 energy as Int64 or Float32?

Int64 at 3204 for anything you bill or compare. Both are in Wh, but the Float32 copy at 45166 stops resolving single watt-hours above 16.8 MWh.

Why does my master get "illegal function" from the PM3250?

The meter supports FC03, FC16, 43/14 and 20 only. FC04 reads and FC06 single-register writes are refused; use FC03 to read and FC16 with a quantity of 1 to write one register.
