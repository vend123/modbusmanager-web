Janitza UMG 96-PA Modbus: the 19000 Block and the +32768 Trick

Janitza's UMG power analysers hold hundreds of Modbus values, but the ones nearly everybody needs sit in one block: the "frequently required measured values" from address 19000, all Float32. Janitza also lets you choose the byte order by address — the same value read 32768 higher comes back little-endian. This page covers the block, the settings and the traps from Janitza's own address list for the UMG 96-PA.

RS-485 Settings

SettingRangeDefault
Device address1–2501
Baud rateAuto, 9600–115200Auto
Data frame1 stop no parity, even, odd, 2 stop no parity8N1

With baud rate on Auto the meter detects the master's speed from the traffic — convenient, but on a quiet or mixed line set a fixed rate. Note the address range runs to 250, beyond the 247 most masters accept. Function codes: 03 and 04 read, 06 and 16 write, 23 read/write, 02 digital inputs, 08 diagnostics.

The Frequently Required Values from 19000

All Float32 (two registers). The address list gives plain addresses — no 4xxxx notation and no offset mentioned — so request 19000. If the first value comes out as half a float, your master counts from 1: enter 19001.

Powers are in watts and energies in watt-hours — a 400 A incomer reads 120 000, not 120. The block has cos φ per phase but no total; divide Psum3 by Ssum3 for that. The same quantities also exist from address 1000 in a different order; use one block, not a mix. Values are refreshed every 200 ms.

Byte Order: Add 32768

Everything in the address list is big-endian at the listed address. Janitza serves the same data little-endian at the address plus 32768: voltage L1-N at 19000 is also at 51768, low byte first. Use that only if your master cannot swap bytes itself — and never mix the two in one poll.

Common Mistakes

  • No answer at first, then fine: baud rate Auto still locking on; set a fixed rate.
  • Power 1000 times too big: W and Wh, not kW and kWh.
  • Garbage floats: started at 19001 on a master that does not count from 1, or the reverse.
  • Byte-swapped nonsense: a little-endian master reading 19000 instead of 51768, or a big-endian one reading 51768.
  • Address 248–250 unreachable: the master stops at 247.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where are the main values in a Janitza UMG 96-PA?

In the "frequently required measured values" block from 19000: voltages 19000–19010, currents 19012–19016, powers 19020–19026, cos phi 19044–19048, frequency 19050 and energies from 19054, all Float32 big-endian.

How do I read Janitza values in little-endian?

Add 32768 to the address: Janitza serves the same data little-endian there. Voltage L1-N is 19000 big-endian and 51768 little-endian.

Why is the Janitza power value so large?

The 19000 block is in base units: power in W and energy in Wh. Divide by 1000 for kW and kWh.

What baud rate does the UMG 96-PA use?

The default is Auto, which detects the master's speed; fixed choices are 9600 to 115200. The default frame is 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit, and the device address 1 (range 1 to 250).

Does the UMG 96-PA give a total power factor?

The 19000 block has cos phi per phase only. Divide Psum3 (19026) by Ssum3 (19034) for the total, as the workspace's script does.
